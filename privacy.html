Privacy policy

Last updated 24 November 2026

Clip Hunter ("the app") is a personal desktop application. It runs only on its owner's own computer, is used only by that owner, and has no servers, website accounts or users of its own. This policy explains exactly what data the app accesses, why, and what happens to it.

YouTube API Services

Clip Hunter uses YouTube API Services. By using Clip Hunter, you agree to be bound by the YouTube Terms of Service. Google's handling of data is described in the Google Privacy Policy.

Google user data the app accesses

The app accesses Google data only after its owner signs in with their Google account and grants permission. It requests three permissions and uses them as follows:

  • Manage your YouTube videos: upload (youtube.upload). Used to upload the video files the owner has approved, with their title, description and tags, to the owner's own channel. No other channel is ever accessed.
  • View your YouTube account (youtube.readonly). Used to read, for videos the app uploaded: the video id, processing and privacy status, whether it is age-restricted, made for kids or blocked in any country, and its view, like and comment counts. Also used to read public data about public Shorts (titles, lengths, view counts) to learn what kinds of clips work.
  • View YouTube Analytics reports (yt-analytics.readonly). Used to read views and average watch time for the owner's own uploaded videos.

The app also stores the OAuth access and refresh tokens that Google issues at sign-in, so it can make these requests. It does not access the owner's email address, contacts, profile, other Google services, or any data about other people.

How the data is used

Google user data is used only to provide the app's features to its owner: posting the owner's approved clips to their channel, showing in the app whether each upload succeeded or was restricted, and comparing how the owner's clips perform so the app can choose and edit better clips. It is not used for advertising, not sold, and not used to build profiles of anyone.

Clip Hunter's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google user data is not used to develop, improve or train generalized AI or machine-learning models.

Sharing

Google user data is not shared with, transferred to or disclosed to any third party. Nobody other than the owner has access to it. The app has no servers: it communicates only directly with Google, Meta and Twitch.

Storage and protection

All data stays on the owner's computer, in the app's own folder, which only the owner's Windows account can read. Data travels only over encrypted HTTPS connections to Google. Sign-in tokens are kept in a separate file that is excluded from backups. Access to the computer is protected by the owner's Windows sign-in.

Retention and deletion

  • Sign-in tokens are kept while the owner uses the app, and deleted within 30 days after access is revoked, or immediately on request.
  • Upload results and statistics about the owner's own videos are kept to improve the clips, until the owner deletes them or asks for deletion.
  • To delete everything, delete the app's folder, or email the address below and it is deleted within 30 days.

Revoking access

  • Google and YouTube: remove Clip Hunter at myaccount.google.com/permissions (Google security settings). The app then can no longer access any Google data.
  • Facebook and Instagram: remove the app under Facebook Settings, Business integrations.

Facebook and Instagram

When the owner connects Facebook, the app asks Meta for permission to list the owner's Pages, post Reels to the owner's own Facebook Page, and publish Reels to the Instagram account linked to it. These tokens are stored and protected the same way, and used only for that.

Twitch data

The app reads public Twitch information only: broadcasts, their public chat replay, and clips viewers made. It does not sign in to Twitch and does not access private Twitch data. Chat usernames are used only to count reactions and are not published.

Children

The app is not directed at children and does not collect data from anyone, including children.

Changes
